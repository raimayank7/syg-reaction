Reaction Test

Reaction test: questions and answers

Short answers to the questions people ask about these tests. The guides go further: start with what reaction time is.

What is a good reaction time?

There is no single answer for a phone or computer test, because the device adds its own delay. With laboratory equipment, healthy young adults typically respond to a light in about 200 to 250 milliseconds. On a screen most people see higher numbers. Compare yourself with yourself on the same device.

Why is my score different on my phone and my laptop?

Screens, touch sensors, mice and browsers each add a delay, and it differs from device to device. Your own reaction time is only part of the number.

Why did I get "Too soon"?

You tapped before the signal. The wait is random so that you cannot predict it. That try is not counted; tap again to restart it.

Is the test accurate?

It measures the time between asking the screen to show the signal and receiving your tap, to the nearest millisecond. That is precise, but it includes the device's delay, so it is not your true reaction time. It is consistent on one device, which is what you need for comparing your own scores.

Can I improve my reaction time?

Practice makes you a little faster at a particular test, mostly in the first sessions, and being rested and alert helps on any day. In sport, people get faster mainly by learning to anticipate, not by speeding up their nerves.

Is the sound test a hearing test?

No. It is a game that measures how quickly you tap after a beep. It cannot assess hearing.

Do these tests show if someone is fit to drive?

No. They are games. They cannot assess driving ability, health or fitness for any task, and must not be used that way.

Where are my scores stored?

In your own browser, on your device, up to thirty recent tries per test. Nothing is sent to us. Clearing your browser data or using another device removes them.

Do the tests work with a mouse and keyboard?

The timed tests work by tap or mouse click. The number memory test uses the keyboard to type the answer.

Are the tests suitable for children?

Yes, as games. Children are slower than adults on average and improve as they grow; do not compare a child with an adult. The ruler drop test and the memory games are good classroom activities.

Does playing memory games make my memory better?

You will get better at the games, mostly by learning to group things. We make no claim that this improves memory or thinking in general.

Why is there no world ranking?

Because scores from different devices cannot be compared fairly, and because we keep no scores at all: there is no account and nothing is sent to us.

The tests

  • Reaction time
    Tap when it turns green
  • Sound reaction
    Tap when you hear the beep
  • Go / no-go
    Tap for green, not for orange
  • Aim
    Hit 20 targets
  • Tap speed
    Taps in 10 seconds
  • Sequence memory
    Repeat a growing pattern
  • Number memory
    Remember longer numbers
  • Tile memory
    Remember which tiles lit up
