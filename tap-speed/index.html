Tap speed test

Tap the box as many times as you can in ten seconds. The clock starts on your first tap.

Loading...

How the test works

  1. Tap the box. The clock starts with that first tap.
  2. Keep tapping for ten seconds. The big number counts your taps.
  3. At the end you get your total and your speed in taps per second.

On a computer, each press of the mouse button counts as a tap, so this is also a click speed test. After the ten seconds the box ignores taps for a moment, so that your last few excited taps do not start a new test by accident.

What limits tapping speed

Tapping fast is not a matter of reaction. It is a matter of how quickly the muscles of the finger and forearm can tighten and relax in turn. For most people that settles at a handful of taps a second with one finger, and it tires quickly: watch the count and you will see your rate fall in the last few seconds. The best scores come from staying loose. A stiff, tense hand taps more slowly, not faster.

Styles of tapping

  • One finger. The plain test. Use the index or middle finger of your stronger hand, moving from the knuckle, with the wrist resting on the table.
  • Two fingers in turn. Alternating two fingers of one hand, or one finger of each hand, roughly doubles the rate, because each finger gets a rest between taps. On a touch screen both fingers must land inside the box.
  • Thumbs. Holding a phone in both hands and drumming with two thumbs is the natural style for many people and often the fastest on a phone.

These are different events, like running and cycling. If you are competing with friends, agree on the style first: "one finger only" is the classic rule.

Fair play

  • No tools: no automatic clicker programs, no vibrating objects.
  • The device stays flat on a table, or is held normally.
  • Same device for everyone if you are comparing, since some touch screens register very fast taps better than others.
  • Best of three attempts, with a rest between them.

Why ten seconds?

Very short tests of one or two seconds reward a lucky burst and are distorted by the start. Long tests of a minute measure stamina more than speed and are tiresome. Ten seconds is long enough to be steady and short enough to stay near your top rate.

Look after your hands

Fast tapping is hard work for small muscles. A few attempts are fun; dozens in a row can leave your hand or forearm aching. Stop if anything hurts, shake your hands out, and come back another day. Do not tense the whole arm to squeeze out an extra tap.

Uses beyond the game

Drummers, tabla players and pianists train exactly this kind of fast, relaxed, alternating movement, as do typists. If the test interests you, try tapping a steady rhythm instead of flat out: can you hold exactly four taps a second for the whole ten seconds? That is harder than it sounds, and closer to what musicians practise.

A game. It is not a test of strength, health or nerve function.
