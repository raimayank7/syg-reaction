Reaction Test

Reaction time test

Tap the box to start, wait for green, then tap as fast as you can. Five tries make one score.

How the test works

  1. Tap the box. It turns red and says "Wait".
  2. After a random pause of between one and a half and four and a half seconds, it turns green.
  3. Tap at once. The time from green to your tap is shown in milliseconds (thousandths of a second).
  4. Do it five times. Your score is the average of the five.

If you tap before the green, the try is not counted and you start it again. The pause is random on purpose, so that you cannot learn the rhythm; you have to react.

What your number is made of

The score is not only you. It is your own reaction time plus the delays of your device: the screen takes a moment to show the green, and the phone takes a moment to notice your finger. On most phones and laptops these delays add several hundredths of a second. So the same person scores differently on different devices, and a number from this page cannot be compared fairly with one measured in a laboratory or on another website. Compare yourself with yourself, on the same device. More on this in why scores differ by device.

What is a typical score?

In laboratory studies with proper equipment, healthy young adults typically respond to a simple light signal in roughly a fifth to a quarter of a second, that is, about 200 to 250 milliseconds. On a phone or computer, with the device's own delay included, most people see somewhat higher numbers. We do not publish a table of "good" and "bad" scores, because we have no way to know your device's delay, and because a single score means little. Your average over several sessions is the honest figure.

How to get your best honest score

  • Rest your finger just above the screen, or on the mouse button, before the green appears.
  • Look at the box, not at your hand.
  • Do two practice rounds first; the first tries of a session are usually slow.
  • Do not try to guess the moment. Guessing gives "Too soon" far more often than a lucky fast time.
  • Use the same device, the same hand and a similar time of day when you want to compare.

Things that change reaction time from day to day

Everyone is slower when tired, distracted or cold-fingered, and faster when alert and warmed up. Practice on this particular test also helps a little, mostly in the first few sessions. A slower day is normal and is not a sign of anything.

This is a game, not a medical or fitness test. It cannot tell you anything about your health, your driving or your fitness for any task.
