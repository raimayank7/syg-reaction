Reaction Test

Go / no-go test

Tap when the box turns green. Keep still when it turns orange. Fourteen rounds.

Loading...

How the test works

  1. Tap to start. Each round begins with "Wait".
  2. After a random pause the box turns either green or orange.
  3. Green: tap as fast as you can. Orange: do nothing, and wait for the next round.
  4. After fourteen rounds you get two results: your average reaction time on the green rounds you answered correctly, and your number of mistakes.

A mistake is a tap on orange, a tap during "Wait", or a green that you missed. About two rounds in three are green. The two colours also carry the words "Tap!" and "Do not tap", so the test does not depend on telling the colours apart.

What it measures

In the plain reaction time test there is nothing to decide: any change means "tap". Here your brain must first identify the signal and then either release the tap or hold it back. Psychologists call this a go / no-go task, one of the simplest forms of choice reaction. Two things are being tested at once: speed, and the ability to stop yourself.

Why you are slower here

Almost everyone's time on this test is longer than on the simple test, often by a tenth of a second or more. The extra time is the decision. This is one of the oldest findings about reaction time: the more possible signals and responses there are, the longer the response takes. With one signal you can prepare the movement completely and just let go. With two, you have to wait and see.

Speed against accuracy

You can lower your time by tapping at the first flicker of colour, but your mistakes will rise. You can make no mistakes by waiting until you are sure, but your time will be slow. This trade is found in every task that asks for a fast decision, and where you set the balance is partly a matter of temperament. A fair way to play: aim for no more than one mistake, and then see how fast you can be.

Everyday examples

  • A batter must decide in a moment whether to play at a ball or leave it.
  • A goalkeeper must not dive for a dummy.
  • A driver at a junction responds to one light and holds back at another.
  • In "Simon says" type games, the whole fun is that stopping yourself is harder than acting.

Tips

  • Say the rule to yourself before starting: "green go, orange no".
  • Keep your finger close, but think of the orange rounds as the ones that matter. Most mistakes are taps on orange made by a finger that was already moving.
  • If you tap during "Wait", slow down; you are guessing.

A game for fun. It is not a test of attention, impulse control or any medical condition, and its result says nothing about you beyond this game on this day.
